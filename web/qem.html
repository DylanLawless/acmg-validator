QEM criteria and outcomes

Each QEM question asks whether something invalidates or weakens the evidence. Negative wording preserves a consistent meaning: FALSE can produce 1 only when the invalidating condition was checked and not found, and qualifying evidence was verified. Missing information contributes uncertainty, not a positive evidence result. These 28 ACMG-derived documentary rules are experimental and do not independently establish biological or clinical correctness.

TRUE, FALSE and NA

Raw answerQEMMeaning
TRUE0The negative check was performed and an invalidating or weakening condition was confirmed.
FALSE1The check was performed, the invalidating condition was not detected, and qualifying evidence was verified and sourced.
NA0The check was not performed, was inconclusive, inapplicable or could not be adequately verified.

TRUE and NA both produce zero, but for different reasons. The raw outcomes remain available in raw_outcomes.tsv. An unchecked question must not be recorded as FALSE merely because no problem was reported.

Recording the check

Each evidence TSV row identifies its ACMG criterion, a generated qem_rule_id and its qem_raw_outcome. Conclusive TRUE or FALSE outcomes require a qem_source_ref, qem_verified and a named, signed-off review. The separate source_ref and verified fields describe ACMG evidence, not the QEM evaluation. Acceptance of an ACMG criterion alone never establishes QEM FALSE.

For example, criterion_PVS1_evidence_unqualified asks whether the null-variant evidence is absent, contradicted or insufficiently substantiated. Documented verification that it is not invalidated permits FALSE → 1; unchecked or uncertain evidence remains NA → 0.
